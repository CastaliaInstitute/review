Castalia Review · How a review works

How a review works

Castalia Review is a series of polyphonic review essays: long-form criticism in which several voices argue with a single book and with one another, always tethered to what the text actually says and culminating in responsible synthesis.

What qualifies a book

The series favors ambitious nonfiction (and occasional fiction) where technology, politics, and culture intersect—works that ask readers to think like citizens of a civilization, not only consumers of opinions. Titles are chosen when they reward slow reading, genuine disagreement, and close attention to argument and evidence.

Three layers in each review

  • Overview essay. A custodial introduction sets stakes and context in a single authorial voice.
  • Faculty marginalia. Quoted passages from the book carry threaded notes from named reviewers. Superscripts in the essay link to those anchors.
  • Synthesis and replies. The main essay closes in responsible synthesis; a separate afterword may gather threads or stage a reply—including, when appropriate, in voce the book itself.

In voce and historical names

Sections headed in voce with labels such as a.plato are not claims that Plato, Durkheim, or Einstein authored the copy. They mark faculty personas: disciplined standpoints Castalia uses to organize disagreement and keep the dramatis personae legible. The prose is contemporary judgment in dialogue form—an intellectual device, not impersonation for entertainment.

Custodian and author of record

Each review names a custodian and author of record who is responsible for editorial integrity: fair quotation, accurate citation of the work under review, and coherence of the whole. The Custodian is responsible for final synthesis and judgment. Faculty voices and staged replies are part of the published method; the custodian remains the accountable editor for the piece as it appears.

How voices are selected

Reviewers are assigned to illuminate different aspects of the argument—e.g. philosophical telos, social structure, scientific ethics—according to what the book puts in play. The point is complementary pressure on the same passages, not a survey of “every possible take.”

Quotation, marginalia, and synthesis

Marginalia pages reproduce short quotations under fair-use practice so readers can see exactly what triggered a note. The main essay may allude without quoting at length; marginalia is where line-level engagement lives. The closing synthesis (and any reply) is answerable to both—the book’s words and the reviewers’ arguments.

As on the marginalia hub for each title: passages are quoted with each reviewer’s comment threaded beneath. Each reviewer page is headed in voce with that reviewer’s faculty id—Castalia / Inquirer house style, shared with The Inquirer—so the convention reads as editorial infrastructure, not a one-off gimmick. Example: marginalia for The Technological Republic.

Review, Inquirer, Institute

Castalia Institute publishes both The Inquirer and Castalia Review. The Inquirer carries the running conversation of the house; Castalia Review publishes discrete, book-length reviews with a custodian, faculty voices, and marginalia. Same publisher, different form—each Review is a standalone publication event.
