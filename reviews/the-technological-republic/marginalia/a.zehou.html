Chapter One — Lost Valley
Silicon Valley has lost its way.

Li Zehou: This is a moral diagnosis disguised as an industrial one. The question is not only where innovation has moved, but what ends now organize technical labor and public imagination.

Chapter One — Lost Valley
Roosevelt intended to see that the machinery of the state… would spur the scientific community forward in service of… public health and national welfare.

Li Zehou: State direction can re-open historical agency, but institutions alone do not produce ethical order. Capacity must be joined to long cultural formation, not only strategic coordination.

Chapter One — Lost Valley
The modern incarnation of Silicon Valley has strayed significantly from this tradition… focusing instead on the consumer market.

Li Zehou: Correct in part. Yet critique of consumption must avoid nostalgia for emergency mobilization. The challenge is to humanize modern technics under normal civic conditions.

Chapter Two — Sparks of Intelligence
A generation of programmers remains ready to dedicate their working lives to sating the needs of capitalist culture… but declines to ask more fundamental questions about what ought to be built.

Li Zehou: The missing category is sedimentation. One cannot command higher purpose by exhortation if schools, workplaces, and everyday media no longer cultivate durable judgment.

Chapter Two — Sparks of Intelligence
The potential integration of weapons systems with increasingly autonomous AI software necessarily brings risks… But the suggestion to halt the development of these technologies is misguided.

Li Zehou: A familiar modern asymmetry: instrumental urgency outruns ethical anthropology. The problem is not merely whether to build, but what kind of subject is formed by the built order.

Chapter Seven — A Balloon Cut Loose
The virtue of a core curriculum… was that it facilitated… the construction of a national identity.

Li Zehou: Civilizational continuity matters, but not as restoration of a static canon. Tradition survives by transformation, not by freezing one historical form as normative.

Chapter Eleven — Builders and Users
Those designing military software were too far and too disconnected from the actual users.

Li Zehou: This is both a technical and ethical gap. Mediation fails when builders are detached from lived consequence; institutions need social thickness, not just managerial metrics.

Chapter Thirteen — A Cloud or a Clock
Ruthless pragmatism.

Li Zehou: Pragmatism without cultivated sensibility becomes one-sided instrumentality: efficient means, impoverished ends. This is the central danger of technical modernity.

Chapter Eighteen — An Aesthetic Point of View
Our collective and contemporary fear of making claims about truth, beauty, the good life, and indeed justice…

Li Zehou: Here the book is closest to the real crisis. Aesthetics is not ornament: it is social education of feeling, the ground from which civic judgment becomes possible.

Chapter Eighteen — An Aesthetic Point of View
The old means of manufacturing a nation… have all but been dismantled or withered from neglect and abuse.

Li Zehou: If older integrative forms have thinned, replacement cannot be merely administrative. New institutions must bind freedom and discipline through shared practices that people inhabit daily.

Chapter Eighteen — An Aesthetic Point of View
Our argument is that the path forward will involve a reconciliation of a commitment to the free market… with the insatiable human desire for some form of collective experience and endeavor.

Li Zehou: This reconciliation is possible only with distributional legitimacy. A republic cannot demand collective purpose while ordinary life becomes materially insecure.

Chapter Sixteen — Piety and Its Price
The reconstruction of a technological republic will… require the resurrection and re-embrace of a sense of national and collective identity…

Li Zehou: National identity can organize will, but must remain subordinate to humane formation. Otherwise the technological republic becomes powerful without becoming civilized.
